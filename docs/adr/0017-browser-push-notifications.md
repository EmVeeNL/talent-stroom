# 0017 — Browser push notifications

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-31
- **Resolves:** —
- **Issue:** #457 (epic #452; follow-ups #463–#465)

## Context

Recruiters need appointment reminders and message alerts while TalentStroom is closed. Epic #452 permits optional browser notifications; #456 owns the in-app notification centre and #235 covers notification preferences. Boutique agencies handle sensitive candidate and client information. AGENTS.md forbids personal data in notification/job payloads, logs, analytics, URLs and shared caches. Browser push introduces browser-selected third-party services and lock-screen exposure even when the app is closed.

This spike changes documentation only. It does not accept this ADR or authorize production changes to authentication, encryption, tenancy, migrations or CI/CD. Acceptance and security/privacy review must precede those changes. The existing [outbox/reminders flow](../flows/06_platform/docs/24_outbox_jobs_reminders.md) remains a design baseline, not evidence of an implemented scheduler.

[Research and measurements](../architecture/engineering/browser-push-research.md) include sources, a reproducible synthetic probe and explicit limits. In local workerd, 1,000 ES256 import/sign/verify operations succeeded, tampered inputs were rejected, and the encryption primitives worked. This establishes feasibility of native Workers cryptography, not a complete RFC 8291 sender or end-to-end delivery. No browser subscription or real provider message was created.

## Options considered

| Option                                         | Privacy                                                                                                            | Permission UX                                                     | Workers / operations                                                                | Devices and revocation                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Generic Web Push, details after opening** | No recruitment content in push; provider still observes routing and traffic metadata; generic lock-screen exposure | Explicit device opt-in after an explanation; never on first visit | Native VAPID signing feasible; sender, retries, secrets and browser tests needed    | Own lifecycle per browser profile/device plus organization preferences; revocation races remain |
| **2. Email/digest only**                       | Email providers also process recipient and delivery metadata; content still needs minimization                     | No browser permission; independent email preferences              | Avoids service worker/VAPID work; mail transport and digest scheduling still needed | Mailbox preferences do not revoke a browser device; less timely and may enter spam              |
| **3. No push**                                 | No additional push-provider disclosure; retain in-app centre                                                       | No prompt or device enrollment                                    | Lowest additional cost and complexity                                               | Nothing to revoke; recruiter must open app or use separately enabled email                      |

## Decision

**Propose option 1 as an optional, default-off supplementary channel for appointment reminders and messages.** In-app notifications remain the source of truth. Preserve independently configured email/digest behavior; do not automatically send email after a push failure or silently enroll users into another channel. Unsupported or declined push must not block the app. Push is best effort, never a guarantee of a timely reminder or proof of reading.

### Payload and display privacy

- Send exactly the fixed versioned application payload `{"v":1,"type":"activity"}` for both use cases (25 UTF-8 bytes before encryption). Reject unknown fields. No names, message previews, appointment time, organization, identifiers, counts, links, action tokens or user locale. Encrypt even this generic payload using RFC 8291; VAPID authenticates the sender and does not encrypt content.
- Display a fixed product title and generic body from bundled `@talentstroom/i18n` keys, with Dutch as source. Proposed keys: `notifications.push.title` (TalentStroom), `notifications.push.body` (Er staat een nieuwe melding voor je klaar.), and settings keys under `notifications.push.*`; final Dutch/English copy belongs to #464. This ADR adds no runtime strings or catalogs. Use the configured locale if locally available, otherwise Dutch; never fetch authenticated data during `push` merely to build a notification.
- Show a visible notification for each delivered push (`userVisibleOnly: true`), coalescing into a constant product-level tag where supported. No silent background synchronization, tracking pixels, content badges, or per-entity tags. Notification click opens/focuses a fixed same-origin app destination. #456/#464 must agree its route; no identifiers or redirect parameters in the URL. Fetch current authorized details only after opening and authenticating. An offline click shows an offline state; expired sessions require sign-in; removed membership reveals no details.
- The browser-selected push provider necessarily receives an opaque endpoint address, sender identity, timing, size and traffic frequency. This is a protocol-routing exception, **not permission to put recruitment data into URLs**. Endpoint addresses are sensitive linkable capabilities, not anonymous data. Protect endpoint/key material in storage; exclude it from logs, analytics, traces, queues and shared caches. No personalization in VAPID claims; use a service contact address. Review provider data flows, retention and transfers before launch; browser permission alone does not resolve those obligations.
- The service worker handles push/click only for this feature. Do not cache authenticated API responses, recruitment records or details for offline notification display. A person looking at the lock screen can still infer TalentStroom usage and activity timing; disclose this before opt-in. No promise of anonymity or EU-only routing.

### Permission and subscription lifecycle

1. Never request native permission on first visit, login, onboarding, page load or a timer. In authenticated notification settings, explain generic content, third-party routing, device scope, lock-screen exposure and revocation. Request only after an explicit enable action. Prepare service worker readiness without losing the user gesture. Feature detect service workers, PushManager and notification support in a secure context. Give iOS/iPadOS Home Screen installation guidance when appropriate, without blocking use.
2. Handle granted, default/dismissed, denied, unsupported and failed enrollment separately. Do not repeatedly prompt; a user can revisit settings voluntarily. Browser denial needs browser-settings guidance, not another native prompt. OS permission is origin-wide; app enrollment and per-type/per-organization preferences are separate and default off.
3. A subscription belongs to one authenticated user and browser profile/service worker registration, with separately authorized organization bindings and preferences. “Device” is a user-facing approximation; do not fingerprint hardware. Never transfer an endpoint to a different account silently. Reconcile `getSubscription()` and permission on signed-in visits, handle renewal/key changes, and do not rely solely on `pushsubscriptionchange` or a non-null expiration time.
4. Server-side registration, listing, preference changes and revocation require authenticated ownership and active organization membership. Before each send or retry, verify current recipient membership, notification eligibility, device enrollment and organization preferences from authoritative state. Never trust a header, subscription ID or cached membership alone. A browser permission grant does not authorize any organization data access.
5. Offer revoke this device and revoke all devices. Revoke server-side first; local disable/logout also unsubscribes the browser and closes existing product notifications where possible. Account switching requires cleanup and fresh opt-in; session/account revocation, organization departure and account deletion invalidate the applicable bindings. If offline logout cannot reach the server, local unsubscribe/cleanup is immediate and server cleanup must reconcile later; do not claim instantaneous remote deletion.
6. Immediately delete revoked/expired endpoint and key material from live storage, cancel eligible pending sends and remove orphan bindings. Proposed retention: expire subscriptions after 90 days without authenticated reconciliation, even with null browser expiry; re-enable only by explicit opt-in. Document backup expiry and minimal non-personal operational retention in #463/#465. Never add endpoint or device identifiers to audit logs.
7. Already accepted/in-flight pushes cannot reliably be recalled after remote revocation. Bound that window with short TTL and generic content; the opened app always reauthorizes. Revoke-all stops future server sends; remote browser OS permission and previously displayed notifications may remain until local/browser cleanup. Document these limits in settings.

### Workers sender and delivery policy

Use native Web Crypto ES256/P-256 signing with a secret VAPID private key per environment. The audience is the validated push endpoint origin; choose a one-hour token lifetime (RFC maximum 24 hours). Keep VAPID signing keys separate from ephemeral message encryption keys. Retain key versions during a planned transition and require new subscriptions for a changed restricted application-server key; emergency compromise disables sending and requires re-enrollment. Secrets/configuration changes require accepted ADR review and generated Workers bindings as usual.

Do not turn the measurement probe into production cryptography. Select a maintained, reviewed RFC 8291 sender with Workers interoperability and test vectors; check exact dependency pins and the seven-day supply-chain rule (ADR 0005). No package is selected or installed by this spike. Validate endpoint/key sizes and an explicit HTTPS provider-origin allowlist, reject credentials/redirects/private or arbitrary destinations, and apply enrollment/send rate limits. Subscription input must not become an SSRF proxy. Provider host patterns need real browser validation in #465.

At dispatch, recheck rescheduled/cancelled appointments and message eligibility. Coalesce duplicate activity locally using internal state without exposing event IDs in the push. Request TTL `min(300 seconds, remaining relevance)` and drop expired work; the five-minute cap is a proposed product tradeoff, not a measured optimum. Use normal urgency initially. Treat 404/410 as terminal subscription failures; bounded retries with jitter/Retry-After for 429/5xx/network failures only while still eligible. Investigate 400/401/403 as configuration/protocol errors rather than endlessly retrying or deleting all devices. No payloads, endpoints or provider response bodies in diagnostics. Aggregate result classes suffice.

Integrate with the approved outbox/idempotency design rather than inventing a scheduler here. Pending work must use only approved opaque references and resolve protected state at dispatch; this spike authorizes no new personal-data job schema. Provider acceptance is distinct from device display or reading. Do not emit per-user delivery/read analytics from push. One event can fan out to multiple opted-in profiles; cost and notification volume grow with that fan-out.

## Consequences

Generic messages sharply reduce lock-screen/content risk and do not require candidate decryption in a service worker. Workers can sign without a separate signing service. Optional enrollment respects recruiters who prefer in-app or email notifications.

We gain sensitive subscription storage, revocation and key-rotation operations, provider egress validation, browser compatibility work and ongoing security/privacy review. Generic copy loses actionable detail; opening the app requires network access and current authorization. Browser/OS policies, offline devices and Focus settings can delay or suppress display. Push does not replace the in-app centre or appointment source of truth. Encryption protects content but cannot eliminate metadata correlation or recover an already-dispatched message.

No production dependencies, authentication, encryption, tenancy, CI/CD or migrations change in this spike. Any schema/storage design must be reviewed against ADRs 0006–0012 and accepted ADR requirements before implementation. This decision remains Proposed until the owner accepts it; production enablement additionally depends on the validation follow-up.

## Rollback

Keep server sending and client enrollment independently disableable. On rollback, disable both, cancel pending push work, revoke bindings and purge subscription endpoint/key material under the documented retention policy. Keep independently configured in-app/email behavior available. Remove/rotate VAPID secrets once the sender is retired; restricted subscriptions require fresh enrollment if reintroduced.

Serve a compatible service worker update that closes product notifications and safely retires push handling/unsubscribes where reachable; do not assume removing JavaScript from the page removes installed workers or OS permission. Offline browsers may retain workers/notifications; previously accepted pushes can arrive until TTL expiry. Continue serving the generic handler during transition to avoid broken old subscriptions; later remove unused worker registration only if it owns no other features. Rollback cannot recall provider-accepted messages or erase provider metadata. It incurs cleanup/support and re-enrollment cost, not a migration back of recruitment data.
